On the stability of proximal operators in Wasserstein spaces under different notions of convexity
with Simone Di Marino and Sara Farinelli. arXiv preprint

The proximal operator is a central tool in variational analysis and optimization. In Hilbert spaces, convexity makes it 1-Lipschitz. In Wasserstein spaces, the geometry is different and comparable stability properties are not fully understood.

In \((\mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d), W_2)\), the proximal operator with step \(\tau>0\) is

\[ \operatorname{prox}_{\tau F}^{W}(\mu) := \mathop{\arg\min}_{\nu \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d)} \left\{ F(\nu) + \frac{1}{2\tau} W_2^2(\mu, \nu) \right\}. \]

We study how different notions of convexity control the stability of this map.

  • For functionals convex along generalized geodesics, we prove local \(1/2\)-Hölder continuity.
  • We introduce convexity along 2-base generalized geodesics and show that it yields a 1-Lipschitz proximal map.
  • We also prove conditional \((1+\delta)\)-Lipschitz estimates near Dirac masses or minimizers.

Langevin for Nonconvex Optimization: Exact, Inexact and Zeroth-Order
with Marco Rando, Lorenzo Rosasco and Silvia Villa. arXiv preprint

Langevin methods add Gaussian noise to gradient descent, helping the algorithm explore nonconvex landscapes. We study their global optimization guarantees under smoothness and dissipativity assumptions.

The exact-gradient Langevin step is

\[ x_{k+1} = x_k-\gamma\nabla F(x_k) +\sqrt{2\gamma\beta^{-1}}\,z_k, \qquad z_k\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d). \]
  • We convert relative entropy directly into objective-value error, avoiding intermediate Wasserstein bounds.
  • We derive non-asymptotic excess-risk bounds for exact and inexact Langevin algorithms.
  • For Gaussian and spherical finite-difference estimators, we obtain explicit zeroth-order function-evaluation complexity bounds.

Convergence of zeroth-order proximal point algorithms in the high-temperature regime
with Hippolyte Labarrière, Cesare Molinari and Silvia Villa. arxiv preprint

We study zeroth-order proximal point algorithms in the fixed positive-temperature regime. Unlike vanishing-temperature analyses, we interpret ZOPPA at fixed temperature as an exact proximal-point method for a smoothed auxiliary objective.

The zeroth-order proximal operator (ZOPO) is

\[ \operatorname{zprox}_{\lambda,f}^{\delta}(x) = \frac{ \mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\lambda\delta I)} \left[ Y\exp\left(-f(Y)/\delta\right) \right] }{ \mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\lambda\delta I)} \left[ \exp\left(-f(Y)/\delta\right) \right] }. \]

ZOPPA then iterates

\[ x^{k+1} = \operatorname{zprox}_{\lambda,f}^{\delta}(x^k). \]
